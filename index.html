The clinician flow Scroll sideways →

Clinician · desktop or phone

1Start the consult and listen

One button starts ambient recording. The bottom bar holds the note template, microphone choice (including Tandem's own mic) and "finish and create". Audio is never stored.

Clinician · bedside

1bSame flow on a phone

Runs in the browser on a phone at the bedside. There is no dedicated device; hardware is limited to microphone accessories and a Philips SpeechMike integration.

Tandem product video
Clinician

2Structured note in seconds

The note is generated into template sections (History, Examination, Social, Comment). Tabs switch between the raw Transcription, the Note and + Create document.

Clinician

3Review, then "Add or adjust" by voice

The clinician reviews the draft and dictates corrections rather than typing. The note carries past history and medications only as spoken in the room, not pulled from the record.

Clinician

4It learns the clinician's style

After the same edit is made several times, a "Memory suggestion" offers to keep it (e.g. "Write in bullet points" for Assessment & Plan). It personalises without a settings page.

Clinician

5Pick billing and clinical codes

ICD-10 and SNOMED codes are suggested from the note and ranked "Most relevant / Others", with a search box for overrides. Country billing codes (e.g. Swiss TARDOC) via partners.

Clinician

6Generate the follow-on paperwork

Fit notes, specialty referrals, advice requests and patient letters come from the same visit. Tandem flags one as "Recommended", which is the closest it gets to suggesting a next step.

Clinician

7One click into the EHR

"Save to record" transfers the note into one of 130+ EHR systems. This is the paid Pro feature and the core of Tandem's moat.

Clinician

8Ask a clinical question

CDS is a chat box ("What would you like to do?") with suggested prompts. Answers are sourced from licensed guidelines and drug references, using the visit note as context.

Clinic admin

9Clinic uploads its own protocols

Local guidelines (e.g. an antibiotic policy PDF) become part of the answer set, so the CDS answers "what does our clinic do?".

Design patterns worth noting

  • One persistent action bar (template · adjust · save) follows the clinician through every screen.
  • Voice-first editing: corrections are spoken, not typed.
  • Quiet personalisation: "memory" learns from repeated edits.
  • Calm, neutral visual language with a single lime accent for the primary action; it reads as a tool, not "AI".

What it means for AMBY

  • Tandem's CDS is lookup on the note. It never reads labs, imaging or history, and gives no differential and no test recommendation.
  • Its inputs are what was said in the room. AMBY's inputs are what the tests show.
  • Don't compete on the scribe. Tandem's notes could be one of AMBY's inputs, which makes Tandem a possible channel partner.

Clinician · inside the EHR

5Clinician sees a pre-built chart

Design patterns worth noting

  • White-label: the patient sees Hartford, Novant or Atlantic, never K Health.
  • Conversation ends in an action: every chat resolves to a time slot, not advice.
  • One thread per visit with Pre-visit / Visit / Summary tabs.
  • Visible safety layer: "The Judge" scores each conversation against the system's policy, with a pass/fail and the clause involved.

What it means for AMBY

  • K Health owns the front door. Its diagnosis suggestions rest on the patient's chat, so they stop where tests begin.
  • It is now heading into specialty care (endocrinology at Hartford), so the open window in specialties is narrowing.
  • Sell to the specialty departments of the same systems, and treat K Health's pre-visit chart as one more AMBY input.

The clinician flow Scroll sideways →

Clinician · phone or web

1Ask in plain language

One search box, no forms. The answer opens with a one-paragraph synthesis, then the grade, then the evidence.

Clinician

2Read the cited evidence

Every claim is cited, and licensed journal figures are shown inline. These content deals (NEJM, JAMA, Springer Nature, NCCN, Sage) are the moat.

Clinician

3Check how strong the evidence is

EvidenceGrade shows an A–D grade and why: the starting grade plus upgrades and downgrades. It makes the reasoning auditable, not just the citations.

Clinician · in the room

4Visits: transcribe and annotate the plan

Ambient transcription writes the note, then annotates each plan item with guideline support. Suggested prompts include "Are there other questions I should ask?" and "Check for contraindications".

Clinician

5Query the patient's own documents

Upload reports (including radiology) and ask across them. This is the first step toward reasoning over the patient's record, though the documents are uploaded by hand rather than pulled from the EHR.

Clinician · inside Epic

6Patient-aware answers inside Epic

A "Patient Context" chip attaches the open chart, so the guideline answer adapts to age and allergies. With MSK's OncoKB, this now reaches genomic alterations.

Clinician → patient

7Hand it to the patient

Any answer can be turned into a take-home page at a chosen reading level ("Why taking your statin matters"), shared by link.

Clinician

8…and keeps adding workflow

Dialer (calls, fax, voicemail), CME/MOC credits, coding help and games. The pattern: give clinicians reasons to open it every day.

Design patterns worth noting

  • Single input, layered answer: summary → grade → citations → figures.
  • Trust made visible: EvidenceGrade, "Finished thinking" and source badges on every claim.
  • Context as a chip: "Patient Context: Owen Evans" is a removable token, not a mode.
  • Serif + orange: an editorial, journal-like visual language that signals "medical literature".

What it means for AMBY

  • This is the one to watch. In nine months it went from literature search to patient context inside Epic to genomic knowledge (OncoKB) at MSK. That is AMBY's direction.
  • The gap today: answers respond to a question; they don't proactively produce a ranked differential and the next test from labs + imaging + genomics + history. Uploaded documents are manual.
  • AMBY must match OpenEvidence's trust patterns (grades, citations, visible reasoning) from day one; clinicians will expect them.

The Ergo Visit flow Scroll sideways →

Clinician · before the room

1Get up to speed from the chart

Before walking in, the clinician asks Art for a summary, grouped by problem. Every point links back to the note it came from. Used 16 million times a month.

Clinician · phone in the room

2AI Charting listens

Started from Epic's own mobile app, already tied to the open patient. A single stop button. During Ergo Visit, it also pulls chart data onto the screen as topics come up.

Clinician

3Orders heard in the visit, ready to sign

"Heard in Visit Recording" turns what was said into real orders (a Bumex dose change, a smoking-cessation referral) linked to the problem. The clinician taps Sign.

Clinician

4Accept the drafted note

A full SOAP note, with a waveform icon marking each AI-drafted section. Structure can be changed by voice ("make HPI a bulleted list"). Accept files it.

Clinician → patient

5Explain results to the patient

When labs and imaging come back, Art drafts a patient-friendly message; the clinician picks "Start with Draft". Memorial Hermann saw 32% fewer follow-up questions.

Patient

6Emmie answers the patient

Patients "Ask Emmie" in MyChart, with answers grounded in their full history. This competes directly with K Health's PatientGPT, which Atlantic Health also put in MyChart.

Design patterns worth noting

  • Zero context switching: the AI starts inside the chart that is already open.
  • AI output becomes real orders, matched to orderables and preference lists, not free text.
  • Provenance markers: waveform icons show which text the AI drafted.
  • Human commit: nothing files until Sign / Accept.
  • Dense, functional Epic UI with a purple-to-pink AI gradient.

What it means for AMBY

  • Epic already does scribe + orders + full-record summary natively, for free with the EHR. Standalone scribes are being reassessed. AMBY cannot be a substitute for Epic; it has to be additive.
  • Epic suggests orders that were said aloud. It does not reason about which test should be ordered or rank a differential across labs, imaging and genomics. That is AMBY's slot.
  • Deliver as a SMART-on-FHIR app inside Epic and copy Epic's patterns: orders as signable objects and a visible "AI drafted this" marker.

For AMBY's own product

Six UI patterns to borrow

These patterns show up across all four products. Clinicians will expect most of them in AMBY on day one.

1

Outputs become signable orders

Show suggested tests as real orders, matched to the system's order catalogue, one tap to sign. Not a paragraph of advice.

Seen in: Epic AI Charting
2

Grade every recommendation

An evidence or confidence grade beside each item in the differential, with the "why" one click away.

Seen in: OpenEvidence EvidenceGrade
3

Patient context as a visible chip

Make it obvious which patient and which data sources (labs, imaging, genomics) are in play, and let the clinician remove one.

Seen in: OpenEvidence patient-aware
4

Mark what the AI wrote

A consistent marker on every AI-generated line, and nothing filed without an explicit human commit.

Seen in: Epic waveform icons, Tandem "Save to record"
5

Learn from edits quietly

When a clinician repeatedly overrides a suggestion, offer to remember it rather than making them configure settings.

Seen in: Tandem "Memory suggestion"
6

End in an action

Every AMBY session should close with a concrete next step (an order, a referral, a booked test), not just insight.

Seen in: K Health PatientGPT
